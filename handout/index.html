Five patients, five stations, one Saturday

A hematopathology workshop for preclinical medical students. At 9:00 they meet five patients. They rotate through five stations, and each station adds one kind of evidence on all five. Nobody knows which patient their team will present until the app draws it. A companion app runs the clock, the cases and the score.

Each station slot is 30 minutes: about 27 at the station and the rest to walk. The stations end at 12:10. Then a one-hour working lunch: teams wrap up all five cases while facilitators and moderators circulate to help, and midway, at 12:40, the app assigns each team the case it will present.

The carousel

Every team visits every station. The order differs, so each station stands on its own.

1 The multi-head scope

A short film on collecting a bone marrow. A normal smear and a normal aspirate. Then the smears and marrows of all five cases.

Morphology · Dr. Wey

2 The CBC and the analyzer

An animation of how the instrument counts. Then how to read a CBC with differential by lineage and what each index means, on the five patients' counts.

Numbers · Dr. Inamdar

3 Flow cytometry

No instrument on site, so an animated film explains the idea. Then each patient's plots: one population or a mixture, and what that means.

Immunophenotype · resident or fellow

4 The clinic

How each patient presents. The chemistries, B12, folate and MMA, LDH, molecular and genetic results, and treatment. How one report integrates every modality.

Clinical bridge · hematology clinician

5 The lab tour: hematology workflow

A short film on how the lab is organized, then a walk through the core lab with the hematology workflow in focus: a tube's path from the inlet to the stockyard, the CBC line, how a smear is made and stained, and CellaVision.

The game. No team owns a case. Every team logs findings on all five in the app, station by station. Midway through the working lunch the app assigns each team the case it will present. Points come from the summative huddle and from Jeopardy. Case 5 is a draft for Dr. Wey to edit; all patients are fictional. Stations are colored circles and cases are colored squares, so the two never mix.

What we have to buildPage 2 of 3 · Objectives, the app, the people

Learning objectives

By the end of the workshop, the student will be able to:

  1. Identify the normal cells of blood and marrow and assign each to its lineage.Station 1
  2. Interpret a CBC with differential by lineage, using the indices, absolute counts and the reticulocyte count.Station 2
  3. Explain how an automated analyzer produces a CBC, and recognize a result that is an artifact.Stations 2 and 5
  4. Distinguish reactive from neoplastic blood findings on a smear, and recognize a blast.Station 1
  5. Describe the principle of flow cytometry and read a plot as one population or a mixture.Station 3
  6. Apply a stepwise approach to anemia and to thrombocytopenia, and choose the next test.Stations 2 and 4
  7. Integrate the history, CBC, morphology, chemistry, flow and molecular results into one diagnosis and one report.Station 4, working lunch
  8. Trace a specimen through the laboratory and name who does what along the way.Station 5
  9. Present a case at a multidisciplinary conference and defend the diagnosis with evidence.Summative huddle
  10. Describe what a hematopathologist contributes to the care of a patient.The whole day

Registration and the surveys

Capped at 30, with a waitlist. Sign-up opens a few months ahead and closes at a deadline. Students past 30 join the waitlist in order. Two weeks before the day everyone confirms, and open seats go to the waitlist.

Surveys and the IRB. A survey before, after, and four to six weeks later. OZB is contacting the IRB office; sign-up opens only after its reply. Answers stay in a simple file of our own.
